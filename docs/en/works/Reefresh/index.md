# REEFRESH – MAYBE NEXT TIME
##### 2026

## Starting point

Deep-sea corals live far beyond our everyday experience. At the same time, climate change, ocean acidification and microplastics are changing their habitats. I wanted to find a way to tell these stories without simply listing facts and fears about the future.

## Idea and story

REEFRESH – MAYBE NEXT TIME takes place in a future in which deep-sea corals have already become extinct. A digital archive looks back at their final attempts to adapt to an increasingly difficult environment.

The corals develop unusual traits. Some might protect themselves against plastic, while others could produce light or take on new forms. The archive presents these ideas as discoveries from a possible future: sometimes serious, sometimes strange and occasionally humorous.

## Many people, one coral

Every participant receives the same drawing of a deep-sea coral on an A4 sheet. They can draw how the coral would have to change to survive in the future. The results range from biologically plausible adaptations to entirely speculative creatures.

The drawings become three-dimensional corals with their own fictional stories. Although everyone starts with the same template, a whole reef of different perspectives emerges.

## Making the work

The contributions are collected in an interactive web archive. Visitors can explore the corals as 3D models and place them within a shared story about the future. The website connects the original drawings, imagined species, environmental changes and a timeline.

The website is finished and live at [reefresh.org](https://reefresh.org/). You can explore the corals and their stories there yourself.

## Context and presentation

The project developed through an exchange with MARUM. From 2 to 5 November 2026, I took part in [BINOMIALS](https://www.9isdsc.csic.es/binomials/), a group exhibition in the Historical Building of the University of Barcelona. The exhibition was part of the 9th International Symposium on Deep-Sea Corals and brought art and deep-sea research together.

The archive grows with each new contribution.

## My contribution and the participants

I developed the concept, future narrative, participatory format, visual design, texts, 3D translations and interactive website. I also designed the logo. The ideas for the individual corals come from the people who draw their own adaptations onto the template. Their different perspectives form the heart of the project.
